# Content voice

Atlas Relics writes as a careful editor, not a guru and not a clinic.

## Do

- Use complete paragraphs a person would say aloud.
- Hold a psychology, reflection, and ancient-wisdom point of view at once.
- Describe practices as ways of looking, not as cures.
- Keep product claims specific: what the object is, how it is used, who it is for.
- Name limits. Readings are not therapy. Maps are not diagnoses. Courses do not promise a new personality.

## Do not

- Use em dashes.
- Use the word “unlock.”
- Use stock AI phrasing (“dive in,” “elevate your journey,” “transform your life,” “in today’s fast-paced world”).
- Diagnose the customer.
- Claim guaranteed awakening, healing, wealth, or relationship outcomes.
- Use occult clichés in copy or art: galaxies, glowing portals, crystals, smoke-wreathed sigils, generic mandalas.

## Names

- **Atlas Relics** is the house.
- **Conscious Mirror**, **The Caves**, and **Pattern Map** are product lines.
- Courses, readings, books, and digital tools are also product lines, not sub-brands.

## Product-line sense

**Conscious Mirror** watches how inner life appears in outer circumstance.

**The Caves** is descent work: slower, older, quieter rooms of the psyche.

**Pattern Map** draws repeating shapes in thought, relationship, and choice without calling them a disorder.

## Example

Poor: “Unlock your highest self and transform your reality today.”

Better: “A workbook for noticing the pattern you keep walking, then deciding what to do with that information.”
